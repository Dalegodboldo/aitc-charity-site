Some musicians spend years chasing a moment like this. Yaffa Botier got hers when a headliner pointed into the crowd and called her up.

## Pulled From the Crowd at Brooklyn Paramount

At a recent Yungblud show at the Brooklyn Paramount, the headliner did something he's known for — he reached past the barricade and pulled a fan up onto the stage. That fan was Yaffa Botier, a nineteen-year-old guitarist. Handed an instrument in front of a sold-out room, she dropped straight into "Fleabag" with the band — no warm-up, no second-guessing. She didn't just survive the moment. She matched it.

![Rock guitarist Yaffa Botier](/images/yaffa-3.JPG)

It's the kind of story that reads like luck. It isn't. Moments like that belong to the players who have already put in the work — and to the people who made sure that work had somewhere to go.

## Meet Yaffa Botier

Yaffa is a nineteen-year-old rock guitarist based on the East Coast. If you follow guitar players online, her name may already be familiar — her playing has a way of stopping the scroll. Her feeds are built on demanding material: Metallica's "Fade to Black," Muse's "Hysteria," Bon Jovi's "Runaway" — the kind of songs that separate players who practice from players who perform. Earlier this year she traveled to NAMM, the music industry's largest annual gathering, where she was featured by Shreddelicious, a platform that spotlights women who play guitar.

But very few players get there on their own. Yaffa's path runs directly through the work we do at the Always In The Club Foundation.

## Mentored by Tony Lucca

Yaffa was mentored by **Tony Lucca** — a singer-songwriter and touring musician who has spent decades making a living on stages and in studios, and who reached the finals of NBC's *The Voice*. He understands the music industry from the inside: how it rewards persistence, how it tests you, and how easily real talent stalls without direction.

That's what makes him a mentor. Anyone can teach a guitarist scales. Teaching someone how to command a room — and how to stay composed while it's watching — takes someone who has done it for real, again and again. That's what Tony gave Yaffa: not just technique, but the steadiness to say yes when the spotlight turns and the band is already counting in.

## Introduced by Chasen Hampton

Yaffa was introduced to our programs by **Chasen Hampton**, a working musician who currently fronts **Close Enemies** — the rock band led by Aerosmith bassist Tom Hamilton. Chasen has built a long career in and around the industry, and he knows what a young artist actually needs to gain traction.

When he came across Yaffa's playing, he didn't simply note the talent — he connected her with the Always In The Club Foundation, where it could be developed with real guidance and pointed toward a lasting career in music.

## The Work Behind the Moment

This is the part of Yaffa's story worth sitting with: the moment on that stage didn't begin on that stage.

It began with a working musician who noticed her and made an introduction. It continued with a mentor who had the experience to develop her. By the time the lights found her in Brooklyn, the preparation was already done — the moment simply asked her to show up ready.

![Yaffa Botier](/images/yaffa-2.JPG)

That is the entire purpose of the Always In The Club Foundation. Through mentoring, coaching, workshops, and hands-on experiential learning, we connect young creatives with working professionals — people who have already navigated the industry and can shorten the path. Our coaches' students have gone on to sign with labels including Epitaph, Capitol, and Universal Music Group, and to perform on stages from Coachella to Lollapalooza.

Yaffa Botier is what that work looks like in practice: a serious young artist, the right guidance, and a moment she was genuinely prepared for.
